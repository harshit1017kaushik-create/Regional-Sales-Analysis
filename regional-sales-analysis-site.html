Predicting margin

The modeling notebook asks a sharper question than "can we predict profit": can anything about who, where or what channel an order came through predict whether it lands above or below a 40% margin — once price and cost are taken out of the equation?

98.99%Accuracy with price & cost included
56.85%Accuracy with only channel, region, product, customer, timing
55.04%Baseline accuracy from guessing the majority class

A logistic regression that sees unit price and unit cost predicts the margin category almost perfectly — unsurprising, since those two numbers define margin by construction. Strip them out and give the model only contextual information, and accuracy collapses to 56.9%, barely past the 55.0% you'd get by always guessing "Low Margin." A decision tree on the same contextual features does even less: 55.6% accuracy by essentially never predicting "High Margin" at all (1.9% recall on that class).

The takeaway: margin in this business is set at the pricing and costing level, not by which customer, region, channel or product is involved. That argues for tightening pricing and cost discipline over trying to chase "better" customers or channels.

What the analysis found

Scale

$1.24B in revenue and $461.74M in profit across 175 customers and 30 products in four US regions, January 2022 through February 2025.

Channel

Wholesale carries the most revenue and profit; Export runs the highest average margin (37.93%) despite the smallest volume. The spread between channels is under a point.

Product

Product 26 leads on revenue and profit; Product 9 leads on margin. The two leaderboards barely overlap — volume and profitability are separate stories here.

Region

West leads on revenue and profit; Northeast trails every region. Margins stay inside a 0.3-point band across all four.

Customer

A few accounts, notably Organon Corp and Colgate-Pa Group, pair strong revenue with above-40% margins — but revenue rank and margin rank move independently across the customer base.

Modeling

Confirms the pattern quantitatively: once price and cost are removed, contextual features barely outperform a coin flip. Margin is a pricing decision, not a targeting one.
